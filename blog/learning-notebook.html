Learning notebook: Obsidian, blog RSS, and auto-translate

4 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

This site is my notebook belajar — a public scratchpad for what I actually set up, not what I read in a tutorial. This entry covers three things I put together recently: Obsidian as the local notes vault, the RSS feed on this blog, and automatic translation of Mandarin content into Indonesian.

1. Obsidian as the notes vault

I installed Obsidian and pointed it at a dedicated vault folder. Notes are plain Markdown files, so the vault is future-proof even if I stop using Obsidian. The point of using it as a notebook is capture speed: write a note the same day I learn something, link related notes with `[[wikilinks]]`, and let the graph show what clusters together.

What helped: keeping one vault for learning notes and a separate folder for project scratch work, so the graph doesn't get noisy. What was hard: resisting the urge to organise folders perfectly before writing anything — the vault only earns its keep once notes actually exist in it.

2. Blog RSS feed

This blog already publishes an RSS feed at /rss.xml, generated by a SvelteKit server route (src/routes/rss.xml/+server.ts) that reads the same posts list the blog index uses. Every new post I add here automatically appears in the feed with its title, link, publication date, and description, so a reader can subscribe in any RSS reader and get new notebook entries without checking the site.

What I confirmed: the feed's lastBuildDate follows the most recent post, each item has a stable guid (the post URL), and dates render as UTC. What I'd add next: per-post categories in the feed so readers can filter, say, only translation or only infrastructure notes.

3. Automatic translation (Mandarin → Indonesian)

The trigger was a Mandarin article I wanted to read in Indonesian. Instead of translating sentence by sentence, I ran the full text through an automatic translation step, then checked the output against the source for the parts that matter (numbers, names, technical terms). Machine translation gets the gist right quickly; the human pass is where accuracy actually lives.

What worked: translating the whole document at once keeps terminology consistent across the piece. What was hard: idioms and industry-specific terms that don't have a clean Indonesian equivalent — those need a manual glossary decision, not another pass through the translator.

What ties them together

Obsidian holds the raw notes, the blog publishes the public versions, RSS delivers them to readers, and translation lets me pull in source material written in Mandarin. One notebook, four steps, and the date stamps when I learned it.
